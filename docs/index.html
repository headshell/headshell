Traditional Services vs. headshell

Two different worlds offered to the music listener.

Company-Centred Model

Spotify / Apple Music

  • Your listening history is held hostage on the platform's server; it is gone when the subscription ends.
  • Year-end summaries (Spotify Wrapped®) come only once a year, and only for the last 12 months.
  • Only the DRM-protected catalog the platform allows can be listened to.
  • Your listening data is processed for advertising and user profiling.
  • When the company changes its terms, every outside tool stops working.
User-Centred Model

headshell

  • The data lives entirely in an SQLite database on your computer; it is never deleted.
  • Unlimited Sleeve cards covering 5 or 10 years, made whenever you like.
  • Your own local FLAC/MP3 archive and your Subsonic/Jellyfin server come together in one place.
  • Zero telemetry, zero surveillance. Works 100% offline.
  • Canonical IDs (ISRC / MBID) keep it from depending on any platform.

Uncompromising Architecture

Engineering that puts user rights and hardware performance at the centre.

Quick Start

Rescue your data in 4 simple steps and listen on your own terms.

01 Request Your History

Ask for your data package from your Spotify account (*Account → Privacy → Extended streaming history*).

# Download the archive that arrives in your email:
my_spotify_data.zip
02 Import and Analyse

Move the archive into a local SQLite database and see your listening statistics instantly:

headshell import my_spotify_data.zip
headshell stats --year 2024 --top 10
03 Make Your Sleeve Card

Create your card in a vertical or square format to share on social media:

headshell sleeve --year 2024 --format story --out story.png
04 Play Freely

Scan your local music folders or connect your Navidrome/Jellyfin server:

headshell provider scan
headshell play "Radiohead" --all --tui

Frequently Asked Questions

All the technical and practical questions you might have about headshell.

Is this a music piracy or MP3 download tool?

Absolutely not. headshell isn't an audio provider but an independent listening identity layer. While you listen to your own legal music archive (FLAC, MP3) or your personal media server (Navidrome, Subsonic, Jellyfin), it frees your history and statistics from the companies' monopoly.

If I close my Spotify account, will I lose my listening history?

Never. From the moment you import your data into headshell once, your whole history is sealed locally in the SQLite database on your computer. Even if Spotify shut down tomorrow or you cancelled your subscription, your ten years of music memory would stay with you.

Do I need to give my Spotify password or an API key?

No. headshell connects to no company's API and doesn't ask for your password. It reads, offline on your own computer, the data export zip you officially downloaded under the EU GDPR's data portability right.

If I delete a music file from my computer, is my listening history deleted?

No. In headshell's architecture, "what you listened to" (the history table) and "what you can play from the disk right now" (the catalog) are kept completely separate. Even if you delete a file, that track's past listening records, scrobbles and statistics stay.

Does it work without an internet connection?

Yes, it can work 100% offline. Importing data, local library search (SQLite FTS5), music playback, the terminal interface and Sleeve card generation need no outside internet connection.

How do I connect my personal Navidrome or Jellyfin server?

You can register it easily with headshell provider add subsonic --url https://... --user yourname. Your password isn't typed on the command line (so it doesn't land in the shell history); it is read from the prompt without echo, and what is kept on disk is a token derived from it.

How is the audio quality? Does it support Hi-Res and lossless?

headshell uses the symphonia decoder and the cpal audio output path, both written in pure Rust. It sends FLAC, WAV, OGG, MP3 and M4A files straight to your hardware as a pure bit stream; it applies no quality loss or compression.

How are songs with broken or missing tags (ID3) matched?

headshell uses a strict canonical identity chain: it resolves songs with, in order, the ISRC code, the MusicBrainz ID, fuzzy matching with duration/artist tolerance, and the audio fingerprint. It is tested on 72 hard, hand-labelled cases with 98%+ accuracy.

How will listening together (rooms) work?

Audio is never relayed from a server (so there is no copyright risk and no server cost). Everyone in a room plays the music from their own local source; only a millisecond-precise time anchor is synced over the network.

Is there a desktop interface (GUI), and will there be a mobile version?

Yes. The Tauri-based desktop interface is already here, with CSS themes users can write. The headshell-core was designed from day one to fit iOS/Android bindings through uniffi; the mobile version comes in a later phase.
